#+TITLE: HashBin: fixed costs

Fixed costs are what you pay to keep HashBin running even if nobody uploads anything. The other
sheets use them for break-even, scenarios and the reserve. This sheet shows each piece, where its number came from,
and what it leaves out. Every number is an input: the =#+CONSTANTS:= lines in the source and the
input columns of each table. Prices checked 2026-10-24. Calc rule: =*= binds tighter than =/=.

* 2. What fixed costs leave out, and why

These grow with use or happen only once, so they aren't fixed yearly costs. The ones that grow
with use are in the pricing sheet's per-GB margin and scenarios instead.

| Cost                        | Price                                          | Why it isn't a fixed cost                                                         |

* 3. What the fixed costs mean

Margin is what you keep from each paid GB-month after R2 and the card fee on a typical deposit.
"Paid GB to cover it" is how much storage users must pay for all year for HashBin to break even.

| Measure                            | Earlier ($81) | Now     | Unit            |

| Share of your $500 loss budget     |             0 |       0 | %               |
| Margin per paid GB-month           |             0 |       0 | $/GB-month      |

* 4. Could the $60 go away?

Workers Paid is three-quarters of the total. HashBin's Durable Objects all use SQLite storage,
which the free Workers plan allows, so the site might run on the free plan for about $20 a year.
The free plan also can't bill you more: past its limits, requests fail instead.

The catch is the free plan's limits: 100,000 requests a day and 10 ms of CPU per request. Hashing
a large upload can take more than 10 ms, and one popular link can use up 100,000 requests in an
afternoon, taking the whole site down until the next day. That's fine while only friends are
testing, and wrong for a public launch. This hasn't been tested; try uploads on a free-plan
preview before relying on it.
