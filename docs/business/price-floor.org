#+TITLE: HashBin: price floor

The question: what must HashBin charge so that the money customers pay is more than what HashBin
pays Stripe and Cloudflare? This sheet counts every Stripe and Cloudflare charge a customer can
cause and nothing else (no Clerk, DMCA fee, insurance or LLC; see the fixed-costs and legal
sheets). The Workers plan and both domains count, because Cloudflare bills them. Every number is
an input: the =#+CONSTANTS:= lines in the source and the input columns of each table. Guesses are
marked "guess". Operation counts come from reading src/api/content.js and
workers/256t-content. Prices checked 2026-10-24.
Calc rule: =*= binds tighter than =/=, so write =(a/b)*c=.

credit + 2.9% + $0.30, but Stripe takes 2.9% of the whole charge, fee included, so a little is
lost on each deposit (section 6). Donations never add the fee. International cards, refunds and
disputes cost the same either way. Rates of international cards, refunds, disputes and donations

* 6. What this found

- *The answer: at least $0.0164 for each paid GB-month, plus enough paid GB to cover $7.01 a month
  for the plan and domains.* At today's $0.03 that takes about 514 paid GB, as long as free
  downloads stay under about 7,000 per paid GB-month. Below that no sensible price works: with
  2 GB stored, HashBin would have to charge $3.50 a GB-month (the Today row).
- *Free downloads are the cost that can outrun any storage price.* Each 256t.us download is a
  Worker request and four R2 reads (.deleted, .disputed, .meta, the file): about $1.78 per million
  once the free allowances are used up. One file downloaded 200 million times a month costs about
  $350 a month. The responses say "cache for a year", but a Worker on its own domain runs before
  Cloudflare's cache, so every download reaches R2. Saving responses with the Cache API, or
  folding the two marker checks into the .meta read, would cut most of those reads. Neither has
  been tested.
- *The per-IP limit is cheap but local.* A request over 30 a minute costs one Worker request
  ($0.30 per million): one IP retrying 100 times a second for a month costs about $80. The count
  lives in each Worker instance's memory, so it isn't global, and a bot using many IPs just gets
  served (the viral rows).
- *Today's checkout fee doesn't fully cover Stripe.* The code adds 2.9% + $0.30 to the credit,
  but Stripe takes 2.9% of the total including that fee: about 1.7¢ short on a $10 deposit. The
  total that comes out even is (credit + $0.30) / (1 − 0.029). See the table below.
- *Disputes are the biggest Stripe cost after the card fee itself.* Each one loses the deposit and
  $15; at the guessed 0.5% that's 1.25¢ of every dollar. International cards (+1.5%) and Stripe
  Tax (0.5% once registered) aren't passed to the customer either.
- *API bandwidth is sold by the GB but costs by the request.* A download through the hashbin.org
  API costs about $2.40 per million however small the file (mostly the row recording when it was
  last served), so selling it at $0.02 a GB loses money on files under about 136 KB. Each purchase
  does round up to at least 1¢.
- *src/utils/cost-estimation.js is out of date.* It charges R2 egress at $0.015 a GB (R2 egress is
  free) and Worker requests at $0.50 per million (now $0.30).
