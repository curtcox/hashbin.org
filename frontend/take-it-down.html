Intimate images shared without consent

If HashBin.org is serving a nude, sexual or otherwise intimate image or video of you that was shared without your consent, we will remove it. This includes realistic fake images made to look like you. This page explains how, as required by the TAKE IT DOWN Act.

Report an image

1. Who can ask

The person shown, or someone authorized to act for them, such as a parent, guardian or lawyer. You don't need an account.

2. What to include

The report form asks for:

  1. the content's ID (CID): the long code after https://256t.us/ in its link;
  2. a statement that you believe in good faith the image shows the person in an intimate way and was published without their consent;
  3. your signature: type your full name;
  4. an email address or web address where we can reach you.

An explanation is optional. You never need to send us a copy of the image.

3. What happens next

  • Right away: the content is blocked everywhere HashBin serves it. Anyone who opens its link gets an error instead of the image.
  • Within 48 hours: we review the report and, if it is valid, delete the content permanently. It stays blocked until we decide, and the report never expires.
  • Every copy: HashBin names each file by its contents, so identical copies share one CID. Removing it removes every identical copy, and nobody can upload that file again. An edited or re-encoded version is a different file with its own CID; report each one you find.
  • If the report isn't valid, for example if the content isn't an intimate image, we may deny it and the content becomes available again. We'll contact you if we need more information.

4. Your privacy

Most reports on HashBin are public. These aren't. The public, and the person who uploaded the content, see only that it was reported for this reason and blocked. Your explanation, links, signature and contact details are seen only by HashBin's administrators. See our Privacy Policy.

5. Images of anyone under 18

Report them with the same form. We remove them and report them to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as the law requires. NCMEC's Take It Down service can also help stop images taken when you were under 18 from spreading on other sites. Adults can use StopNCII.org.

6. False reports

Only report an image you believe in good faith was shared without consent. Knowingly false reports may be unlawful, and we may deny reports that don't meet the requirements above.
